As a Railway Network Controller, I have reviewed the Driver’s position from a network-wide operational and safety perspective. Here is my formal response for Round 2:

### 1. Points I agree with
* **Absolute Ban on Driver IT Troubleshooting:** I strongly agree with the driver that they must never act as IT fallback administrators or attempt to debug software, reboot servers, or use personal devices when a DMI fails. While the train is in motion, the driver's exclusive focus must be safe train handling. Any expectation that a driver can "work around" a technical failure independently is a severe safety hazard.
* **Prohibition of Unilateral Safety Judgements:** I agree that drivers must not make up manual schedules or route authorities on the fly during system outages. When digital systems fail, official authority must flow exclusively through structured channels—specifically, Network Control.
* **The Danger of "Silent" Caching:** We are completely aligned on the risk of trains operating indefinitely on stale cached data during connectivity losses. Unmonitored reliance on cached movement authorities directly threatens my ability to maintain safe train separation.

### 2. Points I challenge
* **The Implied Premise that Complete DMI Failure Requires an Immediate Stop-and-Rescue:** While the driver advocates for strict rulebook fallbacks, an immediate, uncoordinated stop on an active mainline following a DMI failure can create secondary hazards, such as gridlock, rear-end collision risks in dense corridors, or severe cascade delays. While the train *must* come to a safe stop or restriction, the subsequent recovery, shunting, or crawling of that train to the nearest safe siding must be strictly orchestrated and authorized by Network Control, rather than treated as a purely local mechanical event.

### 3. Responsibilities that appear unclear
* **Who Initiates the Fallback Communication Protocol?** If a DMI goes dark or connectivity drops, it remains unclear *who* bears the procedural responsibility to initiate contact. Is the driver required to immediately stop and call control via voice radio, or does the network control system detect the telemetry drop first and prompt the controller to challenge the train? In an emergency, waiting for the other party to call creates dangerous dead air.
* **Authority to Override Cached Data Expiry:** If a train hits the maximum offline buffering time limit while passing through a known dead zone (e.g., a long cutting or tunnel), who holds the precise operational authority to grant a temporary, highly restricted extension versus ordering an immediate stop? This boundary between automated safety cut-offs and controller discretion is ambiguous.

### 4. Risks another stakeholder may have overlooked
* **Network-Wide Cascade Effects of Conservative Driver Strops:** The driver’s natural inclination for safety is to halt when ambiguous states arise. However, if multiple drivers simultaneously execute uncoordinated safety stops due to minor DMI glitches or brief communication hiccups, it will instantly paralyze junctions, block interlockings, and blind my active network view, turning localized technical faults into a network-wide operational crisis.
* **Orphaned Journey States in the Control System:** If a driver stops a train due to a system failure and reverts to voice-only coordination without proper status updates to SmartRail, the central system will continue to display a green, moving train on the active dispatcher board while a stationary train sits unprotected in a block section.

### 5. Any proposal that would create problems for my role
* **Any proposal that bypasses Network Control during manual fallback operations:** If depot supervisors, maintenance teams, or any other entities attempt to authorize a train with a degraded/failed DMI to move onto the active mainline without explicit, logged clearance from the Network Controller, it will invalidate my active conflict-detection algorithms and risk catastrophic path conflicts. 

### 6. Questions that still require an architect or business decision
* **What is the hard operational cut-off rule for total DMI failure on an active mainline?** Does business and safety rule policy dictate an absolute immediate halt and withdrawal of the train, or is there a standardized "crawl-to-siding" fallback mode authorized exclusively via central control?
