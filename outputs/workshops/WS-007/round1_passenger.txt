As the Passenger Stakeholder Agent representing commuters, tourists, elderly passengers, travelers with disabilities, and those traveling with children or heavy luggage, here is my independent position for Round 1:

### 1. What matters from my stakeholder perspective
*   **A single, frictionless transaction:** Whether my journey crosses one operator or three, I want to book it in one place, pay once, and receive a single digital ticket or barcode. 
*   **Guaranteed best value:** As an occasional traveler or someone on a tight budget, I should not have to manually stitch together separate tickets across different operator apps to avoid overpaying.
*   **Protection when things go wrong:** If my journey involves non-integrated operators and a delay causes me to miss a connection, I need to know *who* is responsible for getting me to my destination and compensating me.
*   **Trust and simplicity:** As an elderly passenger or someone traveling with young children, I am easily overwhelmed by fragmented instructions, unexpected redirections to third-party sites, or complicated account creation processes.

### 2. What responsibility my role has
*   To advocate for clear, upfront pricing with zero hidden fees or confusing terms.
*   To ensure that the platform accounts for the physical and logistical realities of travel (e.g., ensuring ticket combinations allow realistic transfer times, especially for passengers with reduced mobility or heavy luggage).
*   To demand absolute clarity on who holds my personal and financial data and how it is protected against breaches.

### 3. What responsibility my role should NOT have
*   I should *not* have to act as a travel agent trying to figure out which independent operators will honor a ticket if a connection is missed.
*   I should *not* be expected to navigate multiple external websites or create separate accounts just to complete a single multi-operator journey that SmartRail recommended.
*   I should *not* bear the burden of understanding complex behind-the-scenes ticketing rules or non-integrated fare logic.

### 4. What risks or failure cases concern you
*   **The "Broken Journey" Trap (CF-042):** SmartRail suggests an optimal route combining non-integrated operators, but buying them separately means if Operator A is late, Operator B invalidates my ticket for the next leg, leaving me stranded with a crying child or heavy bags.
*   **The Redirection Maze (CF-049):** If SmartRail acts as a redirect tool rather than a direct retailer, passengers could be bounced across multiple operator websites, resulting in cart abandonment, session timeouts, double-charging, or loss of applied railcard discounts.
*   **Data Vulnerability and Fatigue (CF-050):** If users are forced to create accounts and input payment details across multiple redirected operator sites, the risk of data compromise multiplies significantly compared to a single secure national account.

### 5. What decision you believe needs clarification
*   **Direct Retail vs. Redirection (CF-049):** We need an immediate and definitive decision on whether SmartRail is a **one-stop shop** or merely a journey planner with links. If it does not sell tickets directly for all routes, it fails the core passenger expectation of a national, unified travel platform.
*   **Liability in Multi-Operator Travel (CF-042):** When non-integrated operators are bundled into a single journey recommendation, who guarantees the connection and passenger rights?

### 6. Any assumption you disagree with
*   I strongly challenge any assumption that "redirecting users to individual operator sites" is an acceptable substitute for direct retail. Forcing elderly passengers, tourists, or parents with children to leave the SmartRail app and complete purchases on fragmented legacy operator websites introduces unacceptable friction, confusion, and customer service drop-off points. SmartRail must own the end-to-end retail experience.
