As the Product Owner Stakeholder Agent for SmartRail, I am presenting my independent position for Round 1 of the workshop.

### 1. What matters from my stakeholder perspective
My primary focus is on maximizing passenger value, ensuring railway operational continuity, and achieving measurable business outcomes. For the MVP, this means rapidly closing the gap on inefficient, siloed incident management so that disruptions are handled swiftly, resources are allocated effectively, and passengers receive accurate, real-time updates rather than remaining stranded or uninformed.

### 2. What responsibility I believe my role has
* Defining and defending the core business objectives, prioritization criteria, and MVP boundaries.
* Ensuring that features directly tie to operational efficiency and passenger trust.
* Protecting the project scope from creeping into massive, non-essential domains (like AI predictive maintenance or dynamic pricing) during the MVP phase.
* Establishing clear, measurable business KPIs (e.g., reduction in incident response time, accuracy of passenger notifications).

### 3. What responsibility my role should NOT have
* I am **not** responsible for designing software architecture, choosing tech stacks, database schemas, or writing technical integration code (CF-062). 
* I should not dictate how engineers achieve performance targets, only *what* the acceptable business latency thresholds are.

### 4. What risks or failure cases concern me
* **Scope Creep:** Sinking MVP resources into complex post-MVP candidates (CF-061) such as automated passenger compensation, full CRM suites, or end-to-end asset management, which will delay core incident response delivery.
* **Communication Breakdown:** Failing to provide clear, timely information to passengers during incidents, leading to reputational damage and diminished customer satisfaction (CF-057).
* **Operational Friction:** Designing incident workflows that look good on paper but do not match the real-world operational realities of train drivers, station staff, and controllers (CF-060).

### 5. What decision needs clarification
We must formally baseline the **MVP feature set versus deferred scope**. Specifically, we need an explicit boundary drawn around legacy integration extent (CF-062): we must decide *which* legacy systems are absolute deal-breakers for day-one operations versus those that can be manually bypassed or integrated post-MVP.

### 6. Any assumption I disagree with
I challenge the open question regarding passenger compensation workflows (CF-061). There is an implication that compensation workflows might be needed early. From a business priority perspective, **passenger compensation workflows must strictly be deferred to a post-MVP phase**. Attempting to automate or integrate financial compensation before we have mastered basic incident logging, resource allocation, and real-time passenger communication introduces unnecessary legal, financial, and technical complexity that will jeopardize the core MVP.
