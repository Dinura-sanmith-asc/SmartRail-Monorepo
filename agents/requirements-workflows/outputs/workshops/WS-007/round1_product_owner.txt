As the Product Owner Stakeholder Agent for SmartRail, here is my independent position for Round 1 of this workshop:

### 1. What matters from my stakeholder perspective
My primary focus is delivering passenger value, protecting the railway organization's commercial and operational interests, and ensuring SmartRail launches as a viable, trusted product without bogging down in architectural over-complication. Specifically:
* **The Retailing Model:** We need a clear commercial boundary. If SmartRail is just a redirection tool, we lose ownership of the customer experience and vital revenue data. If we act as a full direct retailer, we inherit massive PCI-DSS compliance and customer support liabilities.
* **Multi-Operator Complexity:** Passengers expect a seamless "door-to-door" journey, but non-integrated operators introduce immense friction in fare combinations and revenue allocation. 
* **Trust and Security:** Passengers must trust SmartRail with their payment details and personal data, meaning our compliance posture must be airtight from day one.

### 2. What responsibility my role has
* Defining the core product scope and feature prioritization for SmartRail (what goes into the MVP vs. what gets deferred).
* Establishing clear business rules for fare display, retailing boundaries, and customer journeys.
* Balancing passenger advocacy (best-value fares, transparency) with railway operational and commercial viability.
* Setting measurable business outcomes for the platform (e.g., conversion rates, user trust, reduction in fragmented ticketing complaints).

### 3. What responsibility my role should NOT have
* I am **not** here to design the software architecture, payment gateway integrations, or data encryption standards. How the engineering team achieves PCI-DSS compliance or routes API calls to disparate operators is their domain, not mine.
* I do not dictate individual train operating companies' (TOCs) internal commercial policies, though I define how SmartRail interacts with them.

### 4. What risks or failure cases concern me
* **Scope Creep via Multi-Operator Logic:** Trying to solve deep-seated industry commercial disputes and non-integrated fare structures within our MVP could permanently stall the project. 
* **Liability Trap:** Storing raw payment card data or failing to meet strict regulatory privacy standards (like GDPR or local equivalents) could expose the organization to catastrophic financial and reputational damage.
* **The "Directory" Trap:** If SmartRail merely redirects users to external operator sites for final purchase, conversion rates will plummet, and passengers will view the platform as a half-baked directory rather than a true national travel companion.

### 5. What decision you believe needs clarification
We must make an immediate, firm product decision on **retailing strategy**: *Is SmartRail a direct merchant of record for ticket sales (handling checkout and payment in-app), or is it an aggregator/redirect platform?* We cannot design the user journey or commercial agreements until this is resolved.

### 6. Any assumption you disagree with
I challenge the implicit assumption in CF-042 that SmartRail is responsible for single-handedly solving the multi-operator non-integrated ticketing problem for the entire railway network at launch. While we should *display* best-value options where data is available, forcing SmartRail to invent new ticketing products or bridge commercial gaps between uncooperative operators is out of scope for our initial product phase. The MVP must focus on standard, supported integration paths first.
