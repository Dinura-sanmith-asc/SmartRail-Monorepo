As a Customer Service Representative Stakeholder, I have reviewed the Passenger Stakeholder Agent's position for Round 2. Here is my operational response:

### 1. Points I agree with
* **The "Dead Phone / No Signal" Trap (CF-052):** I completely agree that offline tickets and token access are not optional. When passengers get stuck at barriers or can't pull up their journey details due to station signal loss, they immediately flood our customer service desks, frustrated and defensive. We need a reliable fallback so our staff aren't spending valuable disruption-handling time adjudicating digital connectivity issues.
* **The Stranded Connection Anxiety (CF-055):** The Passenger Agent rightly highlights the panic caused by missing a tight connection. Passengers come to us demanding to know if their ticket is valid for the next service. We desperately need clear, definitive rules here so we can give quick, confident answers instead of guessing.

### 2. Points I challenge
* **Unified Compensation Management (CF-056):** The Passenger Agent assumes SmartRail should act as a single, unified window for auto-populated, one-click compensation claims. While I understand why passengers want this, we must challenge whether customer service or SmartRail itself is legally and operationally structured to handle multi-operator financial payouts. If SmartRail takes this on without a clear backend financial settlement process between distinct train operating companies, frontline staff will inevitably get dragged into endless disputes when payouts are delayed or rejected. 

### 3. Responsibilities that appear unclear
* **Who owns passenger guidance when digital systems fail?** The Passenger Agent rightly emphasizes wanting clear instructions on alternative trains during a disruption. However, it remains entirely unclear *who* is responsible for pushing that alternative routing data to us and the passengers simultaneously. If the app tells a passenger one thing, but frontline staff are looking at outdated operational sheets, the responsibility breakdown leads directly to volatile desk confrontations.

### 4. Risks another stakeholder may have overlooked
* **The "Customized Threshold" Overload Risk:** The Passenger Agent suggests allowing passengers to customize their push notification thresholds (e.g., alerting them to 5-minute delays). While great for the individual passenger, this risks creating a massive volume of minor, low-impact inquiries at customer service desks. If thousands of commuters are suddenly pinged about minor 5-minute schedule shifts, many will swarm our help points demanding immediate re-routing alternatives for delays that might easily self-correct, overwhelming our capacity to help passengers facing *actual* catastrophic disruptions (cancellations and missed connections).

### 5. Any proposal that would create problems for my role
* **Any proposal that promises passengers "instant, automated re-booking" via the app without defining how frontline staff override or sync with it.** If an app auto-rebooks a stranded passenger onto a train that is already at 100% capacity, those passengers will still end up at our desks demanding physical seat allocations or alternative accommodations. Automation that doesn't align with physical train capacity or staff visibility will directly sabotage our ability to manage crowds safely.

### 6. Questions that still require an architect or business decision
* **Connection Protection Policy (CF-055):** Are missed connections due to minor inbound delays formally "protected" by default, requiring operating staff to honor onward travel, or will passengers be forced to secure new reservations? We need a hard business rule.
* **Compensation Ownership (CF-056):** Is SmartRail merely an information and redirection portal for Delay Repay, or is it a central financial clearinghouse? 
* **Live Accessibility Status (CF-043, CF-053):** When station infrastructure (lifts/escalators) fails unexpectedly, what is the exact operational feedback loop to ensure customer service screens and passenger apps update *instantly* to prevent stranded passengers?
