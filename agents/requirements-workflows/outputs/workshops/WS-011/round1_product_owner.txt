As the Product Owner Stakeholder Agent for SmartRail, here is my independent position for Round 1 of this workshop:

### 1. What matters from my stakeholder perspective
My primary focus is ensuring that SmartRail delivers measurable business value—balancing network-wide optimization with safe, practical, and accountable station operations. Specifically, I care about:
* **Operational Safety & Accountability:** Local staff must have clear, unambiguous authority to protect their passengers and platforms when automated network recommendations clash with physical realities on the ground.
* **Closed-Loop Asset & Incident Management:** We need a streamlined way to log and resolve local infrastructure issues (e.g., broken ticket machines, spills) without drowning staff in bureaucratic software or expanding the product scope into a full-blown enterprise asset management (EAM) suite.
* **Passenger Trust & Communication:** Public announcements and display messaging must remain accurate, avoiding panic-inducing errors while empowering station managers to inform customers swiftly during disruptions.

### 2. What responsibility my role has
* Defining the core product vision, business priorities, and functional scope for station-level incident management and resilience.
* Determining what features are essential for the MVP versus what must be deferred to later phases to protect the delivery timeline and budget.
* Arbitrating between conflicting stakeholder demands (e.g., network throughput optimization vs. local safety overrides) based on railway safety regulations and business goals.
* Establishing clear, measurable product outcomes for station operations.

### 3. What responsibility my role should NOT have
* I am **not** here to design the technical software architecture, choose specific database schemas, or specify offline caching protocols and hardware backup power specifications. 
* I do not write the code or dictate how engineers implement user interfaces or API integrations.

### 4. What risks or failure cases concern me
* **Scope Creep via Asset Management:** Transforming SmartRail's local incident input into a complex, multi-tiered maintenance workflow tool that duplicates dedicated third-party enterprise maintenance systems. We need simple "report and resolve" loop closure, not a heavy EAM platform.
* **"Alarm Fatigue" and Override Paralysis:** If network optimization algorithms constantly conflict with local platform allocations without a clear hierarchy of authority, staff may either ignore safety warnings or constantly override the network, degrading system-wide efficiency.
* **Panic-Inducing PA Errors:** Allowing unconstrained, ad-hoc text-to-speech or free-typing for public announcements without verification workflows creates severe safety and liability risks.

### 5. What decision needs clarification
* **The Hierarchy of Authority (CF-079):** We must formally establish the operational policy for platform allocation disputes. Does local human safety veto always supersede network optimization instantly? (The business answer must be yes, but the workflow and logging requirements need definition).
* **MVP Boundary for Incident Workflows (CF-083 / CF-084):** We need to define the exact boundary between a simple local incident logger versus complex tiered incident management workflows. 

### 6. Any assumption you disagree with
* **Assumption (CF-088):** There is an implicit assumption that network-wide optimization algorithms should *automatically* ingest real-time local station density metrics without manual intervention. From a product perspective, assuming seamless, automated ingestion for the MVP is risky and likely out of scope. We should rely on threshold-based alerts or manual local context triggers first, treating fully automated density ingestion as a post-MVP enhancement unless specifically mandated by safety regulators.
