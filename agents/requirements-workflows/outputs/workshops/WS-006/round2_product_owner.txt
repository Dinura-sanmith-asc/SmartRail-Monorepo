As the Product Owner Stakeholder Agent for SmartRail, I have reviewed the Train Driver’s input from Round 2. Here is my evaluation from a product, business, and operational perspective:

### 1. Points I agree with
* **Advisory Nature of Braking Guides (CF-035):** The Driver correctly points out that any braking assistance cannot mandate rigid braking curves that ignore real-time adhesion (rails, weather). From a product perspective, precision stopping guidance *must* be strictly advisory for the MVP to avoid catastrophic liability and regulatory pushback.
* **Frictionless Exception Handling for Auto-Population (CF-038):** The Driver’s warning about auto-generated reports is spot on. If the UX forces drivers to wade through bloated logs to sign off, we will destroy turnaround times. We need a streamlined "verify/edit" mechanism, not a bureaucratic text-entry hurdle.
* **System Precedence Rule:** I fully support the Driver's operational hierarchy definition (Control instructions > On-board systems > Paper). The business needs this codified so we don't build software logic that accidentally traps a driver in a "system-says-no" deadlock when Control overrides.

### 2. Points I challenge
* **The Demand for DMI Visibility of External Roles (CF-040):** The Driver states: *"I need to know *who* gave me permission to move or secure the train."* While I understand the operational desire for transparency, we must draw a hard MVP boundary here. If SmartRail's DMI starts pulling in real-time status updates from Shunters, Maintenance crews, and Signallers directly onto the driver's screen, we are building a sprawling enterprise integration beast rather than a focused driver-assist tool. We need to decide if a simple "Cleared for Departure / Secured" binary status from Control is sufficient, rather than displaying granular external role workflows on the DMI.

### 3. Responsibilities that appear unclear
* **Accountability for Data Discrepancy Resolution:** The Driver notes they should *not* be responsible for debugging data discrepancies on the fly between paper, Control, and on-board data. While true, *someone* in the operational loop must reconcile these before a journey proceeds. It remains unclear in our workflow design: when SmartRail flags a data conflict, what is the exact operational hand-off to Control to clear it, and how does SmartRail unblock the driver once resolved?

### 4. Risks another stakeholder may have overlooked
* **Software Freeze vs. Physical Lockout (CF-037):** The Driver rightly fears being trapped on a roster by a DMI freeze during post-journey sign-off. However, the overlooked risk from a *business* perspective is **regulatory audit failure and un-secure trains**. If we build a fallback "emergency bypass" for frozen post-journey workflows so drivers can go home, how do we legally guarantee that the train was actually parked, braked, and powered down safely? We risk trading a roster issue for a runaway train or security liability.
* **Scope Creep via "Nice-to-Know" External Data:** By asking for visibility into *who* gave permission (Shunters, Maintenance), the Driver is inadvertently pushing us toward a multi-user operational platform. If Maintenance is late updating their status in an external system, does SmartRail hold up our train? We must protect the MVP from external dependency bottlenecks.

### 5. Any proposal that would create problems for your role
* The Driver's insistence that the DMI *must* display the clearance states of external roles (Shunters, etc.) creates a major product delivery problem. It threatens to blow up our MVP scope by forcing us to integrate with legacy maintenance and shunting management systems just to populate the driver's screen. From a business ROI perspective, this is low-value complexity compared to core safety, journey logging, and turnaround efficiency.

### 6. Questions that still require an architect or business decision
* **Business Decision:** Do we officially cap the MVP external integration surface area to a single "Control Authority" flag, leaving Shunter/Maintenance clearances as verbal/legacy workflows managed outside SmartRail? 
* **Operational/Regulatory Decision:** What is the mandated organizational fallback protocol if a train's digital post-journey securement checklist fails electronically at an isolated outstation? (i.e., Can it be signed off via phone to Control, or does it require a physical paper logbook entry?)
